<Section id="overview" title="The page asked people to apply and told them nothing worth applying to.">
  <Prose>
    Loop's existing careers page listed open roles. Everything a candidate
    actually decides on was missing: what the company values, how the interview
    process runs, what the benefits are, whether the team is in an office, what
    it is like to work there. Culture, process and benefits were all opaque.

    Loop's own position is that it is transparent in its job descriptions and
    shares salary upfront. The page was not making that case, which is a
    strange thing for a recruitment page to fail at.
  </Prose>
</Section>

<Section id="directions" title="I built three complete pages instead of three mood boards.">
  <Prose>
    Same content and section order across all three: hero, guiding principles,
    meet the team, an FAQ covering the things the old page hid, filterable open
    roles, and a closing block for people who do not fit a current listing.
    They differ in visual treatment, and each commits to one signature
    material rather than mixing.

    One runs on gradient surfaces in Loop's deep green. One uses frosted glass
    panels over photography. One is flat and close to monochrome, letting type
    scale carry the hierarchy. Building all three as real HTML rather than
    static comps meant the FAQ actually expanded, the role filters actually
    filtered, and the scroll behaviour from the hero CTA to the roles section
    could be judged rather than imagined.
  </Prose>

<Section id="decisions" title="Two decisions worth defending.">
  <Prose>
    **The featured teammate rotates.** Leadership photos in a flat grid make a
    company look like an org chart. One person featured large with the rest as
    smaller cards below, rotating on a timer and promoting whichever card you
    click, gives every person a turn at full size without ranking anybody. It
    also means the block reads as people rather than as a directory.

    **The FAQ is the argument, not the filler.** Everything the old page hid
    became an accordion item: what we look for, how we work, what the culture
    is, how we hire. Putting "we give the salary upfront" in a page section
    commits the company to it in a way a job description does not.
  </Prose>

  <Prose>
    What I could not solve: real photography. Every person block is a labelled
    placeholder, and a hiring page whose warmth depends on photos of real
    people is only as good as the shoot that has not happened yet. The final
    build also happens in Webflow rather than in my code, so these three are
    the design argument and not the artifact that ships.
  </Prose>
</Section>

<Section id="reflection" title="Reflection">
  <Prose>
    **Three finished pages beat one page and two directions described.** Nobody
    in review had to imagine anything, and the conversation went straight to
    which one, which is the conversation I wanted.

    **A recruitment page is a product surface.** The FAQ is doing the same job
    as the decline copy in the onboarding redesign: answering the question
    somebody is actually asking, at the moment they are asking it, instead of
    making them find out later.
  </Prose>
</Section>
