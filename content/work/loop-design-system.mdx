<Section id="overview" title="What is Loop's blue, and who gets to answer?">
  <Prose>
    The brief I was given was to build the canonical Figma file: fork the
    Untitled UI kit, restyle it into Loop's house style, publish it as a shared
    library, and give the company somewhere to point when anyone asks what the
    heading style is.

    The problem with that brief is that Loop already had an answer to those
    questions. It was just written in CSS. Four years of shipped product had
    accumulated a real design system in `theme.css` and `variables.css`, and
    the components consuming it were live in front of four thousand
    businesses. A new Figma file would not have been the source of truth. It
    would have been a second one.
  </Prose>
</Section>

<Section id="inversion" title="I inverted the project in the first week, after seven conversations.">
  <Prose>
    I spent my first three days in 1:1s with the frontend engineers, a
    technical PM, and my manager, mapping how design actually reached
    production. What I found was that there was no handoff to speak of.
    Engineers designed in code, and the components drifted from each other
    because nothing held them to a common definition.

    So the direction flipped. Instead of building a Figma library and pushing
    it toward code, I forked *from* Storybook into Figma, component by
    component, and treated every difference between the two as a finding
    rather than an error to quietly fix.
  </Prose>

  <PullQuote>
    The problem was never where the components lived. It was that nothing was
    comparing them.
  </PullQuote>

  <Prose>
    This reframe is the whole project. Putting the two side by side is what
    makes drift visible, and visible drift is fixable drift. Both engineers I
    ran it past backed it the same afternoon.
  </Prose>
</Section>

<Section id="audit" title="Two success greens, one black brand color, and a greyscale numbered backwards.">
  <Prose>
    I compared three sources: the design-system summary I had written as
    forward-looking intent, the live token files in the repo, and an earlier
    DTCG token export. Every value quoted below is from the code, with a file
    and line, because a reconciliation nobody can verify is just an opinion
    with a table.
  </Prose>

  <Figure
    src="/work/loop/design-system.png"

    caption="The summary artifact, rendered in the system it documents. Two of the errors the audit caught are visible in this screenshot: the radius scale reads 14 to 34px when the code tops out at 28px, and the brand ramp is labelled 25 to 900 when the code runs 50 to 950."
    wide
  />

  <Prose>
    What came out of it, in severity order:

    - **Status color forks by component.** The token layer resolves success to
      Tailwind green `#00a63e`. The brand layer defines `--semantic-green`
      `#30ac2e`, and AlertBanner and ErrorState consume that one. Whether
      "succeeded" is one green or the other depends on which screen you land
      on. Same story for the two reds.
    - **Typography disagrees with itself in three places.** The documented
      direction is DM Sans and DM Mono. The code loads Inter and Roboto Mono.
      Articulat CF, the legacy face, is still attached to `<main>`. The type
      *scale* matched perfectly, which is what made the family mismatch easy
      to miss.
    - **Dark Green is a brand primary with no token.** `#02271B` is in the
      brand guidelines and nowhere in the code.
    - **The greyscale is numbered in opposite directions.** Brand guidelines
      say Grey 45 is darker than Grey 35. `variables.css` says the reverse.
      Both are in use.
    - **The semantic layer is doubled.** `--color-bg-*` and
      `--background-color-*` mirror each other for most roles, which is the
      exact drift the project exists to kill, reproduced inside the token file.

    None of this was surprising. A four-year-old codebase with no design owner
    is going to drift, and I would have bet on most of these before I opened a
    file. What made the audit worth doing was not discovering that drift
    existed. It was that half of these are shipping defects rather than
    documentation problems, and you cannot tell which half without checking
    every value against the code.
  </Prose>
</Section>

<Section id="ramp" title="The brand ramp turned the primary brand color into black.">
  <Prose>
    The worst finding needed math to confirm. Loop's brand ramp is not authored
    by hand. It is derived in OKLCH from a single anchor, Loop Green `#045B3F`,
    with each step moving lightness by 0.1 and clamping at the ends.

    Loop Green measures L 0.418. Five steps down from the anchor is L 0.018,
    and six is zero. So `brand-900` and `brand-950` both compute to pure black,
    and `brand-800` lands at `#000d00`, which is black with a rumour of green
    in it. `--color-text-brand-primary` is aliased to `brand-900`. Loop's
    primary brand text renders black.
  </Prose>

  <Figure
    src="/work/loop/brand-ramp.svg"
    alt="Loop's brand ramp as derived versus re-spaced, eleven steps each"
    caption="I recomputed the ramp from the anchor to confirm the crush was in the derivation rather than in how I was reading it. The bottom row is the proposed fix."
    wide
  />

  <Prose>
    The fix is not a clamp. Clamping the bottom of the ramp keeps it black, it
    just stops it going further. The dark end has to be re-spaced with smaller
    per-step deltas so it stays chromatic all the way down, and the natural
    place to land it is the Dark Green that was already a brand primary and
    already missing from the code. `#02271B` measures L 0.243, which is
    *lighter* than the current computed `brand-700`, so this is a genuine
    re-derivation rather than a tweak.
  </Prose>
</Section>

<Section id="library" title="Seven of the conflicts were not mine to resolve.">
  <Prose>
    The audit produced sixteen findings. I tagged each one with who has to
    move: correct the document, correct the code, or an owner has to pick. Nine
    were unambiguous and I wrote the fix. Seven were real forks with no
    technically correct answer, and I did not decide those.

    Whether `natural-grey-*` gets retired in favour of Tailwind neutral is a
    brand call. Which direction the grey numbering runs is a convention that
    other people's muscle memory depends on. Which green means success is a
    decision someone has to still agree with in two years. I wrote them up as a
    pick-one list with a recommendation each and took them to the owner, which
    took longer than deciding unilaterally and is the only reason the decisions
    will hold after my internship ends.
  </Prose>

  <Prose>
    What exists now: an audit of the 260 color tokens in Storybook, a Figma
    variables library with primitives and semantic light and dark mappings
    built from the reconciled values, and light and dark screenshots of every
    staging page mapped back to the Storybook components that produced them.
    The typography change is the first piece to go out internally.
  </Prose>
</Section>

<Section id="reflection" title="Reflection">
  <Prose>
    **Source of truth does not mean correct.** The rule I set was that
    Storybook wins where the two disagree, and it is a good rule, but the ramp
    finding is a case where the source of truth was the thing that was broken.
    Deciding *who has to move* per finding turned out to matter more than
    deciding which artifact ranks higher.

    **The boring findings were the expensive ones.** A duplicate alias family
    and an inverted numbering convention are not interesting to write up. They
    are also what makes a component library slowly stop being a system, because
    each one gives the next person a defensible reason to pick either.

    **Talking to people changed the work more than iterating did.** I inverted
    the entire project after three days of conversations, not after three days
    of design. That has been true of nearly everything I have done here since.
  </Prose>
</Section>
